SEASON HIGHLIGHTS

  • Introduced Benchmark One, an 18-team guillotine league with IDP. Funded by the $5 trophy fee since our trophy manufacturer was affected by tariffs, the cash prize of $120 was winner-take-all.
  • New managers. 1 new manager & 1 returned from hiatus after 13 years.
  • Buy-ins, payouts, and penalties increased. This is the first season that every Comp manager preferred a $100 buy-in.
  • New draft formats. Comp managers were able to select snake draft positions before the draft, and One managers drafted autodrafted teams.
  • Comp's first snake draft. And the exit survey was positive on this.
  • Major changes. 5 point passing TDs. Week 14 Can't Cut List.
  • Minor changes. DST return yards. "Loan trades" banned.

PRIME

Highlights

  • Eugene won his first championship, coming from behind twice in week 17 to defeat Bryan who dominated the entire regular season. Eugene was the 3rd highest scoring team in the regular season, with the 8th hardest schedule. A manager who plays both leagues, he won Comp's 2024 season and fielded a strong roster for most of One's inaugural season.
  • Jeff's hard schedule. A record 3067.01 points against, 60+ points more than last season's record—and 116.51 more than the next hardest schedule. Plus, he scored the 8th most points, so truly the worst luck to land in last place.
  • All of the top 6 scoring teams made the playoffs. Typically, at least one high-scoring team misses the playoffs.
  • Bryan's all-time regular season dominance. Never lower than 3rd in the standings all season, Bryan was in 1st from weeks 3 to 14—an unprecedented run.
  • Gabe's first time missing the playoffs. The two-time Prime champ has made the playoffs every year for the last five seasons.
  • Eugene and Anthony ’s efficiency. Both only made 30 transactions in the regular season, the 2nd fewest in the league, and yet both finished 1st and 3rd, respectively.
  • 18 trades for the entire season. 2 more than last season. Still less than the 24 trades in 2022.
  • 11% of FAAB remaining at the end of the season. In 2024, 11.75%. In 2023, 5.375%.

COMP

Highlights

  • Cory's stealth championship. An honorable-mention “comeback” case in his own right: he started the season in 10th place (for just one week) and still won it all. Even wilder: Cory was in the “Never the best” club (never had the highest weekly score) while also leading the league in matchup sweats (5 tight matchups, winning 4).
  • Reinier’s stat-sheet MVP season. Reinier basically owned the superlatives page: most points (3111.19), most weekly payouts (4 top-score weeks), largest matchup margin of victory (+98.24 on Matt in Week 5), most median wins (10), best waiver manager (33 adds), most active (100 transactions), and Dealmaker (6 trades).
  • James’s rollercoaster season. Comeback Manager of the Year after starting 12th for a month, climbing as high as 3rd by Week 8, then falling out in Week 13—while staying out of penalties trouble… except he also led the league in weekly penalties (4). And somehow James posted both the highest weekly score (270.43 in Week 5) and the lowest weekly score (122.26 in Week 12)… plus another bottom-3 clunker (125.08 in Week 14).
  • Mary’s second-half inferno. The hottest streak of the year: a 12-win run from Weeks 9–14, plus the league lead in matchup wins (11). She also nailed the season’s ultimate squeaker: smallest matchup margin of victory (+0.39 over Mark in Week 2)—and still found time for a 95.63-point demolition of Matt (Week 10), one of the biggest blowouts of the season.
  • Hayden’s median madness. Hayden somehow captured both ends of the median spectrum: the largest median margin of victory (a record-setting 43.15 gap in Week 5) and the smallest median margin of victory (a 0.01 edge over Matt in Week 4). Also: zero trades, joining the season-long trade freeze.
  • Matt’s monopoly of negative superlatives. The Baluser had a brutal full-house of lows: fewest points (2493.79), fewest matchup wins (3), fewest median wins (3), and least active (1 transaction). The only “bright spot” was the easiest schedule (2594.48 points against).
  • Only 8 trades for the entire season. Same number as 2024. In 2023 there were 9 trades. In 2022 there were 13 trades. 7 managers completed zero trades (Gabe, Hayden, James, Kevin, Kyle, Mark, Mary), while Reinier and Eugene did basically all the dealing (Reinier 6; Eugene 4).
  • 29.5% of FAAB remaining. In 2024, 17.04%. In 2023, 11.83%.

ONE

Highlights

  • 18 managers. One survivor. One’s inaugural season combined six offensive starters with six individual defensive players. Four managers were eliminated after Week 4, followed by one elimination each week through the Week 17 final. This was a start/sit league; best ball came later.
  • Defense decided the championship. Hayden’s offense outscored Kyle’s 160.74–148.72, but Kyle’s IDPs won their side 92–56. That 36-point defensive advantage delivered Kyle the title, 240.72–216.74.
  • Kyle’s escape act. He opened Week 1 ranked 14th of 18, then posted the lowest surviving score in Weeks 13, 15, and 16. His Week 16 escape over Kevin came by just 4.00 points. A week later, he won the final 240.72–216.74.
  • Beheaded while leading. Eugene entered Week 8 with the most cumulative points among 11 surviving teams, then was chopped with 144.87. Anthony entered Week 12 leading the remaining seven, then went out with 176.37. A season-long lead offered no protection from one bad week.
  • James’s roller-coaster season. Week 1’s lowest score, 135.04, became 2025 One’s highest score, 293.40 in Week 10, powered by Jonathan Taylor’s 63.10. James also recorded seven bottom-three finishes while at least four teams remained, survived as the lowest scorer left in Weeks 11 and 12, and was finally chopped in Week 14—just 2.80 behind David.
  • David’s photo finish, then the fall. After surviving Week 14 by 2.80 over James, David was chopped the next week with 197.55—16.05 behind Kyle, the lowest-scoring survivor.
  • The Big Chop. Weeks 1–4 cumulative points eliminated Reinier (578.77), Mary (603.78), Cory (639.21), and Raymond (645.11) together. Raymond’s 203.80 was Week 4’s fifth-best score, but the four-week total decided the cut. Bryan survived with 655.64, just 10.53 clear.
  • The Bijan Robinson curse. Five rosters, five eliminations. Matt L. drafted him and was chopped in Week 7; R.J. paid $967 in FAAB to claim him before going out in Week 9. Gabe followed for $544 and a Week 10 chop, then Kevin for $202 and a Week 16 chop. Hayden paid $20 on Christmas Day, then lost the final. That was $1,733 in FAAB payments without a championship.
  • Eugene’s four-week heater. He led the surviving field in Weeks 2–5 with 262.48, 239.88, 226.37, and 235.17, and finished with the best weekly average: 209.02 over eight weeks. He was still chopped in Week 8. In guillotine, a dominant September buys no protection in October.
  • IDP mattered—a lot. Defensive lineup slots supplied 43.6% of all scoring. Mary relied on them most (57.3%); Anthony least (37.9%). Myles Garrett’s 60 for Chris in Week 8 was the second-highest individual starter score in 2025 One, offense included. Kyle’s most productive starter over the season was linebacker Jordyn Brooks, with 335.08 points.
  • Vickrey returned 11,153 FAAB credits. The waiver ledger’s 207 recorded settlements turned $20,832 in winning bids into $9,679 in payments, leaving 46.2% of the league’s original $18,000 FAAB unspent. James’s $606 bid on C.J. Stroud and Reinier’s $597 bid on Justin Fields both settled for $0. Matt L., Mark, Cory, and Raymond spent no FAAB. Separately, Sleeper recorded 521 completed voluntary transactions: 295 free-agent moves and 226 waiver claims. Trading was disabled.
  • Kyle picked the Big Chop’s pocket. After Cory’s elimination, Kyle claimed De’Von Achane, Jaxon Smith-Njigba, and Aidan Hutchinson for actual Vickrey payments of $300, $230, and $143 in FAAB. All became top-five contributors to his season’s starting-lineup points. Across the season, Kyle made 25 first acquisitions within seven days of a player’s prior roster being chopped—the most in One.
  • The waiver pool became the championship roster. Kyle’s final starting offense featured Trevor Lawrence, Christian McCaffrey, Ja’Marr Chase, Saquon Barkley, Trey McBride, and De’Von Achane—a snapshot of how stacked the surviving teams became.
