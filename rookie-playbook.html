Start Here: Benchmark One

One's whole personality is "survive the guillotine." You don't draft players, teams are best-ball only (Sleeper automatically sets your optimal lineup), and rosters get cut in waves until one team is left standing in Week 17.

The format

  • 32 teams, IDP scoring, no kickers and no D/ST. Trading is allowed through Week 4.
  • Best ball only. Sleeper automatically sets your optimal lineup after each week's games finish — there's no start/sit to manage.
  • The draft is a coin flip, not a skill test. When your turn comes, you choose one of 32 complete, prebuilt fantasy rosters rather than drafting individual players. Team-pick order comes from a "100 Yard Rush" random draw, nudged by luck modifiers tied to how promptly you filled out last year's exit survey and this year's scheduling survey.

The guillotine timeline

  1. Weeks 1–8No cuts. Everyone plays.
  2. After Week 4Trading closes. The player-acquisition deadline arrives before the first cut.
  3. After Week 8Cut from 32 to 14 by cumulative score — the league's biggest waiver event of the year, as 18 full rosters (300+ players) hit free agency at once. Budget your FAAB for it.
  4. Weeks 9–10Two-week "wild card round" among the 14 survivors.
  5. After Week 10Cut from 14 to 8. Surviving rosters expand to a bigger lineup (see below) the same Tuesday.
  6. Weeks 11–16One team is chopped every week.
  7. Week 17Final two teams play for the championship.
If scores are tied at a cut: bench points break the tie first, then the highest single-player score, then a 100 Yard Rush coin flip. For the cuts after Weeks 8 and 10, the first two tiebreakers use cumulative results from that phase.
⚠️ A chopped team's entire roster hits waivers — but the release is held until Tuesday's stat corrections are final (as late as Wednesday night in a close case), so everyone gets a fair shot at the pickups.

Scoring

  • Offense uses standard Benchmark scoring: 5-pt passing TDs, points-per-first-down, TE premium (see below).
  • Defense (IDP) uses a simple tiered scale — 1pt assisted tackle / QB hit, 2pt solo tackle / tackle-for-loss, 3pt pass defended / forced or recovered fumble / blocked kick, 6pt interception / sack / safety / touchdown.

Rules Every League Shares

Applies to One too

Waivers: continuous, and a Vickrey auction

  • $1,000 FAAB in all three leagues. (Sleeper's UI shows a $2,000 ceiling — that's just headroom for FAAB acquired in trades, not your starting budget.)
  • You pay the second-highest bid, not your own. If nobody else bids on a player, the pickup is free. Ties go to whoever has worse waiver priority (see below).
  • Players are on waivers continuously except during game-day lock windows. A typical week: waivers clear at midnight Thursday and Sunday (then free agency the rest of that day, plus all of Monday); Friday, Saturday, Tuesday, and Wednesday are locked, with the next clear at midnight Wednesday-into-Thursday.
  • Waiver priority = reverse standings, recalculated every Tuesday. Before the season starts, One uses reverse team-pick order.
Vickrey edge cases: a losing bid sets the price only if it could actually have won the player; claims that fail for budget, roster, drop, or claim-order reasons do not count. Tied top bids produce no refund. Refunds are provisional until the next waiver clear, and a later refund never retroactively rescues a claim you could not afford when it processed.

The AI co-commissioner: Gudetama

A bot handles two mechanical jobs across all three leagues: computing Vickrey refunds after every waiver clear and updating waiver priority weekly.

  • Every action it takes is posted in league chat — that's the audit trail.
  • Ian (the human commissioner) can reverse anything it does, and any manager can question a bot action in chat.
  • A bot mistake is treated exactly like a commissioner mistake: fixed and appealed the same way.

Scoring philosophy

All three leagues score offense the same way: 0.5 PPR + 0.5 points-per-first-down + a TE reception/first-down premium, plus a 5-point passing touchdown. Team defenses (Prime/Comp only — One uses IDP instead) score on a scale designed so a great defense never outscores a great skill player, but still separates elite units from bad ones across a season.

📜 Best interest clause: if something breaks that the rulebook doesn't cover, the commissioner can act as needed — consulting owners or calling a vote if it's a close call. This backstops everything, including the new bot.
03

Comp Addendum

For managers also in Comp

Comp runs like a normal head-to-head league, layered with Benchmark's waiver/scoring rules above. Here's what's different from One.

Draft & roster

  • Snake draft with third-round reversal. Pick clock: 60 seconds, dropping to 30 at the halfway point.
  • 12-team league. Starting lineup: 1 QB, 2 RB, 3 WR, 1 TE, 1 Superflex, 2 Flex, 1 K, 1 DST. Bench: 8. Best ball, same as One — no start/sit.

Every week is worth two wins

Beat your opponent and land in the league's top half in scoring, and you go 2–0 that week. Split the two, and you go 1–1. Miss both, and it's 0–2. This softens the luck of the schedule.

💰 Weekly prize: the highest score wins $10, paid by that week's lowest score.

Trades

  • No review period — trades process immediately. Reversing one after the fact needs 5 votes in a 12-team league.
  • You can trade FAAB, make offers that expire, and do 3+ team deals. "Loan trades" — sending a player back and forth between the same two rosters — are not allowed, even with FAAB attached.
  • Trade deadline: Week 11.

Playoffs & payouts

  • Top 6 make the playoffs; top 2 seeds get a bye. The bracket reseeds after round one if there's an upset.
  • Making the playoffs guarantees your $100 buy-in back — that's the whole point of the payout design.
  • The bottom 6 are roster-locked once the regular season ends, so no one out of contention can strip the waiver wire.

04

If Things Go Weird

A game gets canceled or rescheduled
It's scored as-is. Only exception: the final round of the playoffs, where a loss only counts if the trailing team would've needed an above-average finish from remaining players to come back — otherwise it's ruled a tie.
Week 14 "Can't Cut" list
You can't cut a player in Week 14 if you wouldn't have cut him in Week 4 — i.e., he's been on your roster 30+ days, he's injured but expected back within 2 weeks, or he's on bye. Stops managers from gutting the player pool during the playoff push.
The season gets shortened or canceled
Payouts, penalties, and refunds get prorated by completed weeks out of the scheduled 14-week season, and the commissioner publishes the math.
